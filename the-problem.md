# A governance gap hiding in plain sight.

Distributed compute platforms now route AI training and inference workloads to independently operated nodes. What started in data centers is moving to the edge. And the edge increasingly means private homes. There is no security standard, no privacy obligation, no zoning classification, no liability framework, and no regulatory pathway that applies to a residential node running AI workloads.

### THE COMPUTE GAP

Proposals for AI oversight including hardware telemetry monitoring and training run verification are designed for centralized facilities. Residential nodes fall entirely outside their scope.

### THE ENERGY QUESTION

Where a node gets its power matters for sustainability, cost, and governance. The energy source for a residential node determines its regulatory exposure and its grid impact.

### THE POLICY VOID

Who is responsible when a residential node processes a workload that causes harm? What standards apply? Who enforces them? None of these questions have answers yet.

## This is what HomeNodes is built to address.

The governance frameworks being built today will shape how AI is overseen for decades. Getting residential nodes into scope now — before the infrastructure pattern is too embedded to govern — is the goal.
