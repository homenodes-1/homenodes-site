# Not a random choice.

Medicine Hat, Alberta is one of the few cities in Canada that owns and operates its own electric, natural gas, and water utilities. That single fact changes what is possible here. The first HomeNodes proof of concept is being built in a single residence in Medicine Hat — not because it is convenient, but because no other city in Canada offers the same combination of municipal energy ownership, solar resource, and accessible governance relationships.

### MUNICIPAL UTILITIES

The City of Medicine Hat operates its own electric, natural gas, and water services. This gives the city direct visibility into residential energy consumption at a granularity that most municipalities simply do not have. A residential compute node that draws additional power is visible to the utility. That feedback loop is essential for governance research.

### SOLAR AND WIND

Southern Alberta receives more sunshine annually than almost anywhere else in Canada — over 2,500 hours per year. Combined with consistent prairie wind and an expanding municipal renewable portfolio, a residential compute node here can be powered by a genuinely low-carbon energy mix. That makes the energy data meaningful, not theoretical.

### THE RIGHT SIZE

Medicine Hat has approximately 65,000 residents. Large enough to be a meaningful sample. Small enough to move quickly. City departments, utility managers, and elected officials are accessible. Decisions that would take years in Calgary or Edmonton can happen in months here. That makes it the right place to test an idea before it scales.

## Medicine Hat is the lab. The framework is for everywhere.

The governance gaps documented here apply to any city where residential compute nodes operate. HomeNodes starts in Medicine Hat because the conditions are right. The goal is a framework that works globally.
