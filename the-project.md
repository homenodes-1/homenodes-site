# A Phased Research Build

HomeNodes is not a thought experiment. It is a working research project with real hardware, real energy data, and a real governance output. The work happens in four phases, starting with a single home in Medicine Hat and expanding from there.

![Cross-section of a house showing a residential AI compute node in the basement, connected to utility power, a firewall, and the internet, with the five governance domains labelled where they apply.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/homenodes-1/homenodes-poc/main/diagrams/homenodes-house-diagram.png)

*How the proof-of-concept node sits in a home. The purple tags show where each of the five governance domains applies. Planned design. Hardware has not been purchased yet.*

### PHASE 1: PROOF OF CONCEPT

A single residential home in Medicine Hat is being configured as a functional AI compute node. The hardware stack mirrors the specifications used by distributed compute platform operators while remaining within the scope of a normal residential electrical service. The node will operate on at least one distributed compute network, monitor power draw against the municipal utility baseline, and document every governance gap encountered during setup and operation.

### PHASE 2: CITY EXPANSION

Once the proof of concept is documented, the project will recruit additional Medicine Hat residents to operate nodes under a research protocol. The goal is five to ten homes representing different residential energy profiles — all-gas, solar-supplemented, and grid-primary households. This phase surfaces governance questions that cannot be answered from a single node.

### PHASE 3: GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

Data from Phases 1 and 2 will inform a published governance framework covering five domains: minimum security standards for residential nodes, privacy obligations under Canadian law, municipal zoning and land-use classification guidance, liability allocation between node operators and platform providers, and regulatory compliance pathways.

[View the framework documents on GitHub](https://github.com/homenodes-1)

## Phase 4: Publication and policy engagement.

The framework will be published as a freely available document and submitted for peer review. Findings will be presented to the IEEE Computer Society, shared with the BlueDot Impact AI safety community, and made available to Canadian policymakers working on compute governance and AI oversight infrastructure.

[See the AI Safety Case](https://homenodes.ca/ai-safety)

## Current status: Phase 1 underway.

The Medicine Hat proof of concept build is in progress. Hardware is being specified and procured. Energy monitoring will be configured as part of the build.
