# A Phased Research Build

HomeNodes is not a thought experiment. It is a working research project. The measurement protocol, budget, and first governance findings are published, and the proof of concept node is the next step. The work happens in four phases, starting with a single home in Medicine Hat, followed by a small multi-home study.

![Cross-section of a house showing a residential AI compute node in the basement, connected to utility power, a firewall, and the internet, with the five governance domains labelled where they apply.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/homenodes-1/homenodes-poc/main/diagrams/homenodes-house-diagram.png)

### PHASE 1: SINGLE-NODE STUDY

One consumer GPU node runs in one Medicine Hat home for sixteen weeks, eight of them listed on a public compute marketplace. The study measures what a host can see about the workloads on their hardware, whether power and network data can identify those workloads, how much energy the node uses, and every point where existing rules give the household no clear answer. The method is fixed and published before the node is listed.

[See Phase 1 in detail](https://homenodes.ca/phase-1)

### PHASE 2: MULTI-HOME STUDY

Once Phase 1 is documented, the same measurements are repeated in five to ten Medicine Hat homes with different energy profiles: gas-primary, solar-supplemented, and grid-primary. Every home receives identical hardware on loan, under a research protocol that is independently reviewed before anyone is recruited. This is a research sample with a fixed end date. It shows which findings hold across households and which belong to one home.

[See Phase 2 in detail](https://homenodes.ca/phase-2)

### PHASE 3: GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

Data from Phases 1 and 2 will inform a published governance framework covering five domains: minimum security standards for residential nodes, privacy obligations under Canadian law, municipal zoning and land-use classification guidance, liability allocation between node operators and platform providers, and regulatory compliance pathways.

[View the framework documents on GitHub](https://github.com/homenodes-1)

## Phase 4: Publication and policy engagement.

The framework will be published as a freely available document and submitted for peer review. Findings will be presented at a technical conference, shared with compute governance researchers, and made available to Canadian policymakers working on compute governance and AI oversight.

[See the AI Safety Case](https://homenodes.ca/ai-safety)
